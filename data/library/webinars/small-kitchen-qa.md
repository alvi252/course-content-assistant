# Webinar Q&A: Batch cooking in a small kitchen

Transcript of a live Q&A, lightly edited. Questions come from attendees; the answers are Mira's.

## "My kitchen has two burners and almost no counter. Is batch cooking even possible?"

Yes, and small kitchens often do better at it, because you can't make a mess on six surfaces at once. Use the oven as your second worker: one tray of vegetables and one tray of tofu or chicken roasting while a single pot of grains or lentils simmers on the hob. That is three components with two burners to spare.

For counter space, put a board across the sink. It sounds silly until you try it. Cook in waves: oven trays first, because they take longest, then the pot, then the sauces at the end while everything cools.

## "How do I stop my partner eating the components as snacks?"

Give them their own snack box. I'm serious. Label one container "snacks" and fill it with the ends of the roasted vegetables and a bit of the sauce. Label the others with the day they are for. People respect a label far more than a request.

## "I live alone. Doesn't batch cooking mean eating the same thing all week?"

Only if you cook meals instead of components. Cook a smaller base, and on Sunday night freeze half of it flat in a bag. Then use the five directions for the half you keep: a bowl on Monday, a wrap on Tuesday, something fried on Wednesday. The frozen half becomes a head start for the week after.

## "What is the one tool you'd buy first?"

A big, heavy sheet tray and a kitchen timer. Not a gadget. The tray lets you roast a lot at once without steaming everything, and the timer keeps your Sunday at ninety minutes instead of four hours.

## "Honestly, I hate cooking. Will any of this work for me?"

It is built for you. The whole point of batch cooking is to cook less often, not more. If you hate cooking, the 90-minute Sunday is the only cooking session of your week that needs any thought. Everything else is heat, build, finish.
