# Lesson 3: Leftovers as ingredients

## The second-life rule

A leftover is not a meal you already ate. It is an ingredient you already cooked. My second-life rule is simple: every leftover gets a new shape before it gets eaten again. Roast chicken becomes a filling, rice becomes fried rice, and stew becomes a pie or a pasta sauce.

## The leftover shelf

Give leftovers one shelf in the fridge, at eye level, and let nothing else live there. When you open the fridge on a weeknight, the leftover shelf is the first thing you see, so it is the first thing you use. Leftovers hidden behind the milk become science projects.

## Three questions for any leftover

Ask three questions. Can it be crisped? Can it be wrapped? Can it be loosened into a soup or a sauce? For almost every leftover, one of the three answers is yes, and that answer is tonight's dinner.

## Know when to let go

Not every leftover deserves a second life. If nobody liked it the first time, a new shape won't save it. Write it in your kitchen notebook as "not again" and move on without guilt.
