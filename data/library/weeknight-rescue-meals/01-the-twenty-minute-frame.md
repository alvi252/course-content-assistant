# Lesson 1: The 20-minute frame

## Dinner has a shape

On a weeknight you don't need a recipe, you need a shape. My twenty-minute frame has three steps: heat, build, finish. Heat the pan and whatever needs warming. Build the plate from your components. Finish with one fresh thing and one sauce. If a dinner can't fit those three steps, it belongs on a weekend.

## Start the pan first

The first thing you do when you walk into the kitchen is turn on the heat. Not take off your coat, not check your phone, not open the fridge and stare. A hot pan or a boiling kettle buys you minutes while you decide what dinner is going to be.

## The one-pan limit

On a weeknight, one pan and one board. Every extra pan is extra washing, and extra washing is what makes people give up on weeknight cooking. If a dish needs three pans, it is a Sunday dish, however good it looks in the photo.

## When twenty minutes is too long

Some nights you have five minutes and no patience left. That is what the quick zone in your pantry map is for. Eggs on toast with chilli oil is a perfectly good dinner, and choosing it on purpose is part of the plan, not a failure of it.
