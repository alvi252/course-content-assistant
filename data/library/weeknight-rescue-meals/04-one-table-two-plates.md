# Lesson 4: One table, two plates

## Cook one meal, serve it two ways

If someone at your table eats differently from you, whether that is a fussy child, a partner who hates spice or a friend who doesn't eat meat, you don't need to cook two dinners. Cook one meal and serve it two ways. The base stays the same and the finish changes.

## Build the plate at the table

Put the components on the table separately and let everyone build their own plate. The plain version and the spicy version come from the same pot. People eat more happily when they get to choose, and you wash fewer pans.

## Keep one safe thing on the table

For anyone who is wary of new food, keep one thing on the table they always like, such as bread, rice or cucumber. Nobody has to eat the new thing. Knowing there is something familiar nearby is often what makes trying the new thing possible.

## Allergies are not preferences

Preferences can be handled at the table. Allergies can't. If someone has an allergy, plan the whole meal around it from the start and keep their portion separate from the moment you start cooking. When in doubt, ask them or their family what they need, and follow that.
