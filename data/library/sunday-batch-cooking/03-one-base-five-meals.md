# Lesson 3: One base, five meals

## The base is the boring part, on purpose

A good base is plain. Cooked rice, roasted potatoes, a pot of lentils, a tray of roasted vegetables, plain shredded chicken or baked tofu. If you season the base heavily on Sunday, every meal made from it tastes the same by Wednesday. Season lightly with salt, then let each night's flavour do the work.

Students often tell me their bases taste dull. That is the point. A dull base is a blank page.

## The five directions

From one base, I teach five directions. Bowl: base, something fresh, something crunchy and a sauce. Wrap: base rolled up with a sauce and whatever greens are left. Soup: base simmered in stock with a tin of tomatoes or beans. Fried: base crisped in a hot pan with an egg on top. Salad: base cooled, dressed with something sharp and mixed with raw vegetables.

Learn the five directions and you will never again look at a container of rice and feel uninspired. You don't need a new recipe. You need to pick a direction.

## A worked example: one tray of roasted vegetables

On Monday the tray goes into a bowl with rice and a yoghurt sauce. On Tuesday it becomes soup with stock and a tin of beans. On Wednesday it goes into wraps with hummus. On Thursday it is fried with the leftover rice and an egg on top. Same tray, four dinners, and none of them feels like leftovers.

## How much base to cook

Cook enough base for the nights you planned, plus one extra portion. The extra portion is your insurance for the evening when plans change or someone stays for dinner. If you never use it, freeze it on Thursday and it becomes next week's head start.
