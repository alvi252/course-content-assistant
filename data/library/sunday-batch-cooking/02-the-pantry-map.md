# Lesson 2: The pantry map

## Three zones

Your kitchen already has a system. It just isn't written down. The pantry map gives it three zones: the base zone, the flavour zone and the quick zone.

The base zone holds the things a meal is built on: rice, pasta, oats, dried lentils, tinned beans, tinned tomatoes and stock. The flavour zone holds what makes a meal taste like something: spices, vinegars, soy sauce, mustard, jars of pesto or curry paste, and citrus. The quick zone holds what you can eat in five minutes with no cooking at all: eggs, bread, yoghurt, cheese, nuts and fruit.

## Eye level is for what you want to use

Whatever sits at eye level gets used. Whatever sits behind it gets forgotten and bought again. Put the flavour zone at eye level, because flavour is what turns plain components into different dinners. The bases can live lower down. You know they are there.

I learned this the hard way. When I moved house I found four jars of cumin at the back of one cupboard, each bought because I couldn't see the one before it.

## The restock line

On the inside of a cupboard door, keep a short list called the restock line: the ten things you never want to run out of. When you use the last of one, you write it on the shopping list straight away, not later. Later is how you end up with no onions on a Tuesday.

My own restock line has rice, red lentils, tinned tomatoes, onions, garlic, eggs, lemons, soy sauce, chilli flakes and a block of hard cheese. Yours will be different, and it should be.

## Using your photos

Look at the three photos you took after Lesson 1. Circle anything you have two of because you forgot the first one. Circle anything that has been open for months. Those circles show you exactly where your map needs work, and they are usually in the flavour zone.
