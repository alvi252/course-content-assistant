# Lesson 5: Sauces that rescue the week

## Why sauces matter more than recipes

If your components are plain, sauces are where the variety comes from. Three jars in the fridge can make the same base taste like three different cuisines. That is why I would rather you learn three sauces well than thirty recipes badly.

## The three-jar rule

Every Sunday, make or refresh three jars: one creamy, one sharp and one with heat. Creamy might be a yoghurt and garlic sauce or a tahini sauce. Sharp might be a lemon and herb dressing or a quick pickle liquid. Heat might be a chilli oil or a spicy tomato sauce. With those three jars, a bowl, a wrap and a salad from the same base will all taste different.

## The tahini sauce I teach first

Whisk tahini with lemon juice and a pinch of salt. It will seize up and look completely wrong. Keep whisking and add cold water a spoon at a time until it turns smooth and pourable.

That moment when the sauce looks broken and then comes back is the first kitchen lesson most of my students remember. It teaches patience better than anything I could say.

## When a sauce goes wrong

Too sharp, add a little oil or something sweet. Too flat, add salt before you add anything else. Too thick, add water, not more oil. Taste after every change, not after three changes, or you will never know which one helped.
